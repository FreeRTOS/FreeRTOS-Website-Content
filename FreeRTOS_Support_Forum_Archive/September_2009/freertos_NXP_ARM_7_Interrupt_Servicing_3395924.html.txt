</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">NXP ARM 7 Interrupt Servicing</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/anotherant">anotherant</a> on September 10, 2009</div><div class="thread-post-content">Specifically, the 19 posts states the problem and the solution is a &#8216;few weeks away&#8217;. Basically here is the gist of the problem &#40;which, your right, sounds very much like the NXP documented Spurious Interrupts chapter&#41; :
&quot;The actual reason for all the sporadic occasional RTX failures you have been seeing is most likely due to the NXP LPC2xxx VIC undocumented &quot;feature&quot; &#40;described bellow&#41; and that RTX was not aware of this.
VIC behavior: After an interrupt is disabled &#40;writing to VICIntEnClr&#41; the interrupt is not immediately blocked but can still happen for a few cycles &#40;time needed for VIC to process the request&#41;. Special tests were performed which confirm this behavior.
This &quot;feature&quot; was not taken into account by the RTX kernel. Therefore in some rare situations &#40;very timing specific&#41; it could happen that a blocked interrupt was still executed which eventually lead to RTX failure. Such situations are very rare &#40;can happen sooner when the system time tick interrupt happens more often&#41; and even less likely when the MAM is disabled because then an instruction fetch takes longer then the few cycles that VIC requires. This explains also why the problem was not detected sooner and why it was almost gone when MAM was disabled.&quot;
</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">NXP ARM 7 Interrupt Servicing</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/richardbarry">richardbarry</a> on September 10, 2009</div><div class="thread-post-content">Ok &#8211; reading this again here I see I misread it the first time.&nbsp; It is talking about the VICIntEnClr register, which I don&#8217;t think the FreeRTOS kernel itself ever writes to.&nbsp; Presumably they &#40;being Keil&#41; provide a library for setting and clearing interrupt enable bits, and this is the code that they are going to change?&nbsp; FreeRTOS does not have an equivalent, the interrupt enable and disable registers are at the mercy of the application developer.&nbsp;
