I am very new to using a RTOS. I am investigating a possibility of using a FreeRTOS for the future projects in our company as it has been recommended to us.   
Having almost fully read the &amp;quot;Using Free RTOS…&amp;quot; book I have got a question for you. If in one of our projects we had to process a very fast interrupt &#40;by fast I mean that it could occur very frequently&#41; and we also wanted to still use the RTOS API functions it would have to be defined at a priority lower than the Kernel. This means that jitter will be intoduced to the ISR processing. Can anybody tell me what this jitter is going to be on a PIC32 processor &#40;in clock cycles&#41;?  
I am just trying to evaluate how likely it is that we would have to configure that interrupt at higher priority than the Kernel and not use the API.  
Thank you in advance,  
Konstantin

Konstantin
</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">Kernel Processing time on PIC32</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/richardbarry">richardbarry</a> on September 25, 2009</div><div class="thread-post-content">The PIC32 itself disables interrupts when an interrupt is taken, and you have to process a few instructions before you are able to enable interrupts again.&nbsp; If you look at the code you will be able to count the number of instructions between interrupt entry and interrupts being re-enabled and therefore work out the time &#8211; but I can tell you that its in the order of a couple of hundred nanoseconds.&nbsp; This time is only for interrupts of higher priority than the currently executing interrupt though.&nbsp; You could decrease the time by removing interrupt nesting support &#40;this fact is one of the reasons why comparative performance stats are normally meaningless as nobody actually measures the same thing&#41;.  
As to your actual question about how long equal or lower priority interrupts could be masked by critical sections, I&#8217;m afraid I don&#8217;t have those numbers.&nbsp; Such numbers are generated for SafeRTOS ports, but not FreeRTOS.  
Regards.

</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">Kernel Processing time on PIC32</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/richard_damon">richard_damon</a> on September 27, 2009</div><div class="thread-post-content">While Richard would need to confirm this, I believe the design policy of FreeRTOS was to minimize the periods of interrupts being disabled, even if it might require a somewhat slower algorithm. For the longer periods when he needs to avoid a reschedule, he uses another method of disabling the scheduler, and checking for issues when the scheduler is restarted.
