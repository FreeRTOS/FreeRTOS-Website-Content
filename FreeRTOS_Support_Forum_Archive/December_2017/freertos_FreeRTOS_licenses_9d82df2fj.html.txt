The real step up, as I see it, will be the TLS stuff under MIT license.
 People have been able to use FreeRTOS with LWIP for many years, and
there are at least a couple of usable FAT implementations under
commercial embedded-friendly open source  licences.  TLS has been the
missing part &#8211; you have had to buy a licence (fine for some people and
projects, less good for others) or switch to a completely different
system like mbed.
</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">FreeRTOS+* licenses</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/rtel">rtel</a> on December 15, 2017</div><div class="thread-post-content">The TLS library in the FreeRTOS kernel download (from SourceForge) is a 
third party product so we can&#8217;t change the license.  The TLS in Amazon 
FreeRTOS (from GIT) is mbedTLS (Apache license) with a TLS abstraction 
layer on top.
</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">FreeRTOS+* licenses</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/heinbali01">heinbali01</a> on December 15, 2017</div><div class="thread-post-content">Hi David,
<blockquote>
  People have been able to use FreeRTOS with LWIP for many years,
</blockquote>
That is true but, FreeRTOS+TCP has several advantages above FreeRTOS &amp; lwIP.  Yes the combination worked but there were (I had) many complaints, mostly because lwIP was not written for a multi-tasking system. But also, lwIP is only fast (efficient) when you do NOT use BSD sockets.  FreeRTOS+TCP only has a BSD socket interface which is totally optimised.
In short: there were many good reasons to develop a proprietary UDP/TCP/IP library that is totally integrated with the FreeRTOS kernel.
And better: FreeRTOS+TCP is <strong>already</strong> available under the liberal MIT license!
( actually, the FreeRTOS+TCP library has been tested by individuals at the MIT )
<blockquote>
  and there are at least a couple of usable FAT implementations under
  commercial embedded-friendly open source licences.
</blockquote>
FreeRTOS+FAT will get an MIT license as soon as there is time to move it from /Labs to &#8220;mainstream&#8221;.
The only comparable embedded-friendly FAT driver that I know of is ELM by ChaN.  It is great code, more compact than +FAT.
But the advantage of FreeRTOS+FAT is that it much easier to understand.  Also its error-handling is very complete and helpful.
It has stdio-like user-functions and it mounts several drives into a single root <code>"/"</code>.  For instance, a file like &#8220;<code>/ram/settings.txt</code>&#8221; can be handled by a RAM-disk driver.
<blockquote>
  TLS has been the missing part &#8211; you have had to buy a licence (fine
  for some people and projects, less good for others) or switch to a
  completely different system like mbed.
</blockquote>
I totally agree with this statement.  It would be nice to find a TLS library that has the same liberal license.

enters close cooperation with a large company, but everything I have
