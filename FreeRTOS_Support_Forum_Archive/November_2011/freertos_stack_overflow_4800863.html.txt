</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">stack overflow</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/richard_damon">richard_damon</a> on November 9, 2011</div><div class="thread-post-content">Note that the vApplicationStackOverflowHook is called with 2 parameters to help figure out what happened, the first is the TCB of the task that failed, and the second is the task name.   
Note that tasks can sometimes take differing amounts of memory depending on the path they take, and on many implementations which interrupts occured while they are running. 
</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">stack overflow</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/jandle">jandle</a> on November 9, 2011</div><div class="thread-post-content">Keep a global dubug structure and for each task take the lesser of the measurement and remaining stack.<br />
Place the test at places where you might allocate temporary variables like a char array that chew up stack briefly but not permanently.&nbsp; Allow extra stack for called functions, ISR&#8217;s etc.  
unsigned short usNew = uxTaskGetStackHighWaterMark &#40;xTH&#95;Serial0Command&#41;;<br />
xDebug.usSD&#95;Serial0Command = xDebug.usSD&#95;Serial0Command&lt;usNew?xDebug.usSD&#95;Serial0Command:usNew;

But I can&#8217;t work out why &#8220;systick timer&#8221; stop will cause stackoverflow.<br />
If MCU go to sleep, How to make RTOS keep going after MCU wakeup? Connect the &#8220;systick timer&#8221; to 32Khz oscillator with<br />
same Tick&#95;Rate&#95;Hz&#8221;&#40;say 10mS per tick&#41;, then switch to high frequency oscillator after wakeup?
</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">stack overflow</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/richardbarry">richardbarry</a> on November 10, 2011</div><div class="thread-post-content">I can&#8217;t answer specifically, just generically, but would say it sounds like you are entering a very deep sleep, and therefore it depends on what happens to other peripherals/RAM etc while asleep, and what happens when you wake up again.  
I&#8217;m not sure how you are wanting your system to work, but if the SysTick is stopping, than task blocks times that are not infinite are probably not going to function correctly, as to them, time will have stopped.  
This might be of interest to you &#8211; in particular the pdf file:<br />
<a href="http://interactive.freertos.org/entries/20291196-energy-optimized-port-of-freertos-6-for-efm32">http://interactive.freertos.org/entries/20291196-energy-optimized-port-of-freertos-6-for-efm32</a>
