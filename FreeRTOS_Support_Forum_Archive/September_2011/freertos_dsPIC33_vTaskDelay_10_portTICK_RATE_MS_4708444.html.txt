</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">dsPIC33 vTaskDelay (10/portTICK_RATE_MS);</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/davedoors">davedoors</a> on September 16, 2011</div><div class="thread-post-content">No, vTaskDelay&#40;5000/portTICK&#95;RATE&#95;MS&#41;; is correct, although I take your point that it is probably a badly named constant. thanks&#33;  
</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">dsPIC33 vTaskDelay (10/portTICK_RATE_MS);</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/richardbarry">richardbarry</a> on September 16, 2011</div><div class="thread-post-content">If configTICK&#95;RATE&#95;HZ is set to 1000, then it is true, it does not matter if you divide or multiple &#8211; but that misses the point of the portTICK&#95;RATE&#95;MS constant.&nbsp; portTICK&#95;RATE&#95;MS is provided so you can change configTICK&#95;RATE&#95;HZ without effecting the actual time that any task blocks for.&nbsp; In that case, you must always divide, otherwise it won&#8217;t work if you change the tick frequency &#40;any other tick frequency will result in a portTICK&#95;RATE&#95;MS that is not equal to 1&#41;.&nbsp;   
It should also be noted, that portTICK&#95;RATE&#95;MS only works if configTICK&#95;RATE&#95;HZ is less than or equal to 1000.  
The HZ on the end of the configTICK&#95;RATE&#95;HZ name denotes that the value is specified in HZ.&nbsp; Hence, setting it to 1000 gives you a 1000Hz tick rate.  
The MS on the end of portTICK&#95;RATE&#95;MS denotes that it is in MS.&nbsp; Hence, a tick rate of 1000Hz &#40;1KHz&#41; gives you a tick period of 1ms.&nbsp; portTICK&#95;PERIOD&#95;MS would be a better name.  
Regards.
