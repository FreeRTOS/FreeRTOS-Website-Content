</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">Failed to understand the pointers movement in Queue related operations</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/heinbali01">heinbali01</a> on January 28, 2016</div><div class="thread-post-content">Have a look at xQueueGenericReset() :
~~~~~
    pxQueue->pcWriteTo = pxQueue->pcHead;
    pxQueue->u.pcReadFrom = pxQueue->pcHead +
        ( ( pxQueue->uxLength &#8211; ( UBaseType_t ) 1U ) * pxQueue->uxItemSize );
~~~~~
When the queue is created, the <code>pcReadFrom</code> member will point to the last item in the queue (to <code>N-1</code>)
After that the code will:
~~~~~
    read from ++pcReadFrom  ( pre-increment )
    write to pcWriteTo++ ( post increment )
~~~~~
Does that make it clear?

</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">Failed to understand the pointers movement in Queue related operations</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/heinbali01">heinbali01</a> on January 29, 2016</div><div class="thread-post-content"><blockquote>
  The &#8220;Front&#8221; means the pcTail side, and the &#8220;Back&#8221; means the &#8220;pcHead&#8221; side, am I right?
</blockquote>
No, not necessarily. <code>pcHead</code> and <code>pcTail</code> are a kind of const pointers that define the beginning and the end of the queue space.
Items are written at the position <code>pcWriteTo</code> or <code>pcReadFrom</code>, depending on the value of <code>xCopyPosition</code> (resp BACK or FRONT).
As I said, adding items to the BACK is the usual way. That works like a fifo. Add items a-b-c, and you can read them back in the same order a-b-c.
When you add items a-b-c at with <code>SEND_TO_FRONT</code>, you will read back c-b-a. The last item comes out first.
In some situations, a mixture of <code>SEND_TO_BACK</code> and <code>SEND_TO_FRONT</code> can be very useful: the first one is used for all normal messages, the latter is used to send an emergency message, which will be treated first.
