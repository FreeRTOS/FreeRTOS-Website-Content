  <main class="main-content"><div class="thread-post"><h2 class="thread-post-title">PIC18 using MCC18 footprint sizes?</h2><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/jcwren">jcwren</a> on October 21, 2009</div><div class="thread-post-content">Does anyone have any numbers for what the footprint of FreeRTOS is on a PIC18 part?&nbsp; I realize, of course, that this will be dependent on what options are enabled, but I&#8217;m hoping in addition to the footprint, you might mention with what options.  
I need to roughly size a project where FreeRTOS has been suggested.&nbsp; At the moment, I don&#8217;t have MCC18 available where I can generate a simple project and find out for myself.&nbsp; So any numbers someone can throw at me would be helpful.&nbsp;   
The numbers for any of the demo programs in the PIC18 examples would work well, too.  
Thanks,<br />
-jc
</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">PIC18 using MCC18 footprint sizes?</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/richardbarry">richardbarry</a> on October 21, 2009</div><div class="thread-post-content">Not an answer to your question….but just to point out that the PIC18 is not an ideal target for any pre-emptive kernel.&nbsp; One of the main problems is its segmented memory space, and what to do when a task stack staggers a segment.&nbsp; The PIC24/33 and PIC32 are good targets however.  
Regards.
</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">PIC18 using MCC18 footprint sizes?</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/jcwren">jcwren</a> on October 22, 2009</div><div class="thread-post-content">Oh, I&#8217;m fully aware of that.&nbsp; You know I like I FreeRTOS, and if you&#8217;ve been unlucky enough to be exposed to my rants on PICs &#40;18&#8217;s and below, haven&#8217;t used the newer ones&#41;, you&#8217;d know I&#8217;d rather be programming a 4004 system with DIP switches.&nbsp;   
But when you have people writing specs who have no grasp of how inefficient a PIC is, no idea about the compiler quirks, and no idea what &#42;really&#42; goes into an RTOS, you have a disaster waiting to happen.&nbsp;   
I&#8217;m pretty sure that with most of the options we need enabled we&#8217;re looking around a minimum of 30K of code space &#40;shooting from the hip here&#41;, and enough RAM for very few tasks.  
Looks like I&#8217;ll have to compile up a version and see.&nbsp; If I do, I&#8217;ll post my results back to the list.  
-jc
