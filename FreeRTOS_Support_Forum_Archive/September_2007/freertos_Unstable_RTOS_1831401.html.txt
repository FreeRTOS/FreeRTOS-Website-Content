</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">Unstable RTOS</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/davedoors">davedoors</a> on September 25, 2007</div><div class="thread-post-content">I think the FreeRTOS port is solid and suspect your problems relate to the processor.&nbsp; There has been a lot written on the LPC2000 Yahoo group about the LPC23xx parts.&nbsp; I think NXP have had to rework the die and are just in the process of ramping up production again with parts that actually work.
The FreeRTOS port is used on many different ARM7 parts as does SafeRTOS and they are all basically the same as far as the RTOS goes.&nbsp; It seems to be just the LPC2368 that has problems.

</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">Unstable RTOS</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/richardbarry">richardbarry</a> on September 27, 2007</div><div class="thread-post-content">I don&#8217;t think this issue is related to FreeRTOS.org, but the hardware setup in relation to you chip.&nbsp; You will most likely have the same problem if you use the same hardware setup without FreeRTOS.org.
There was a post this morning saying that if the MAM mode was set to 0 &#40;zero&#41; then the problems went away.&nbsp; Have you tried this?
According to the information I have the issues with the LPC devices are such that the error can show up in an apparently random way, as it is due to where instructions are placed in flash.&nbsp; I cannot confirm this to be the case or not myself as as luck would have it my hardware seems to work ok.
