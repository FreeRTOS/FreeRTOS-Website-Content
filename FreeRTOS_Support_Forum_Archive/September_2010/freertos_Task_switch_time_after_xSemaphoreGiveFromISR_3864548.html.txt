  <main class="main-content"><div class="thread-post"><h2 class="thread-post-title">Task switch time after xSemaphoreGiveFromISR</h2><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/*anonymous">*anonymous</a> on September 24, 2010</div><div class="thread-post-content">I am using xSemaphoreGiveFromISR&#40;&#41; and portEND&#95;SWITCHING&#95;ISR&#40;&#41; to activate a task from a ISR. I have some timing issues, and wish to know what time I could expect from the ISR til my task starts to run from where it was waiting with xSemaphoreTake&#40;&#41;.  
The MCU I am using has a Cortex M3 core and is running at 80 MHz.
</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">Task switch time after xSemaphoreGiveFromISR</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/richardbarry">richardbarry</a> on September 24, 2010</div><div class="thread-post-content">Assuming there are no other interrupts pending, and assuming there is no interrupt nesting, then portEND&#95;SWITCHING&#95;ISR&#40;&#41; on the Cortex M3 will just set a bit in the register.&nbsp; Then when your interrupt exits the software interrupt will run &#40;with tail chaining&#41; and select a new task.&nbsp; When the software interrupt exits your new task will be running.  
I&#8217;m not going to provide the exact details &#40;there are 26 architectures supported, with 55 ports, that would be a full time job&#41; but can recommend that you either measure using a scope &#40;or something similar&#41; or that you just count the cycles used by the output asm instructions.  
Regards.
</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">Task switch time after xSemaphoreGiveFromISR</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/*anonymous">*anonymous</a> on September 27, 2010</div><div class="thread-post-content">Then I suppose the scheduler is not my problem. I am using a semaphore to delay a task, as described above. However, often the time from the ISR til the task gets unblocked may be as long as 150 ms &#40;&#33;&#41;. And this is when I am testing with the highest priority task, and no other tasks that have any work to do.  
Is this a known behavior to you? I am confident that I am doing something wrong, but am not able to find out what it is.
</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">Task switch time after xSemaphoreGiveFromISR</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/richardbarry">richardbarry</a> on September 27, 2010</div><div class="thread-post-content">150ms is an astronimically long time &#8211; probably 600 times what I would expect it to be in the region of.  
What is your tick rate?<br />
Are you using any function such as printf&#40;&#41; that take a long time to complete?<br />
Do you have any critical sections that could take a long time complete?<br />
Most importantly of all &#8211; how are you measuring the time?&nbsp; Sorry to say that normally when people complain of timing issues the problem lies in how they are measuring it &#8211; normally because of incorrect assumptions.  
Regards.

&gt; &#40;and presumably whenever i call<br />

No, it will never be called when portEND&#95;SWITCHING&#95;ISR is called, but portEND&#95;SWITCHING&#95;ISR will cause it to execute. This is described in post 2 of this thread. Also, if you look at the implementation of these functions and macros you will see that this assumption is not right.  
&gt; so I am lead to think that the problem is related to<br />
&gt; the semaphore/queue and/or how the scheduler decides that the task that was<br />

It works for everybody else, so either you have discovered a latent bug or the problem is in your own code. If you are using 10KHz and don&#8217;t understand how the macros work I think it is the later but am ok with you proving me wrong.  
&gt; Initially I measured the delay with a timer on the MCU, but I also checked using<br />

&gt; the delay on such a magnitude that it is easy to see without the analyzer, as<br />
&gt; the GPIO&#8217;s have LED&#8217;s attached to them.  
I think the delay is likely to be because of the 10KHz tick. Do you have stack overflow trapping turned on?

It is also worth pointing out that, although the scheduler will run with a tick rate above 1KHz, if you go above 1KHz then the constant portTICK&#95;RATE&#95;MS will not work.&nbsp; If you are using portTICK&#95;RATE&#95;MS then that could be the cause of the described behaviour.
