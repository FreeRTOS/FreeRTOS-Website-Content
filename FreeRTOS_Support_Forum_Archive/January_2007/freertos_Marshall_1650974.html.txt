</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">Marshall</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/nobody">nobody</a> on January 15, 2007</div><div class="thread-post-content">Even more to the point, Linux is a kernel, not an &quot;OS&quot; in the sense that Windows XP/OS X are operating systems.
</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">Marshall</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/imajeff">imajeff</a> on January 15, 2007</div><div class="thread-post-content">Marshall, I want to try to help explain too what an OS is. First of all, a fully working &quot;Linux&quot; does not even have to have a &quot;screen&quot;, and therefore nothing to look at &#40;unless you mean post the file &quot;tasks.c&quot; or some source code. Shoot, even GNU/Linux might not be complete with a display, even though the intention of GNU is to have at least a text console.
Yet, FreeRTOS is a fully funcional OS in my terms, that by nature, an OS does not reqire any specific feature that another OS has. &quot;OS&quot; describes the surrounding software functionality to support system hardware. If the system has a display, then sure, the OS should include support.
The reason FreeRTOS does not include support for it &#40;I call it minimal OS&#41; is because the author has no clue what type of display I will put in my embedded system, nor anyone else. I have currently only 8 LEDs for my display. Want a snapshot of that? Try looking in the ports. Many of them include a picture of their board and display.
