I just checked the +TCP SAM4E driver:
It is impossible to make the RX-path zero-copy. The EMAC uses fixed-size (128-byte) buffers for reception.
It is possible to make transmission zero-copy: the <code>gmac</code> driver has some application-hook <code>GMAC_USES_TX_CALLBACK</code>.  But, the call-back from the driver to the application is being done from within the ETH interrupt. That is against the idea of a deferred interrupt handler: let the ISR wake-up a task (<code>prvEMACHandlerTask</code>), and let the task check the DMA descriptors.
Also, the ISR <code>gmac_handler()</code> will become quite long when <code>GMAC_USES_TX_CALLBACK</code> is enabled. I would consider rewriting the interrupt handler.
