</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">Tick rate + serial comm.</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/jaydmdigital">jaydmdigital</a> on December 4, 2007</div><div class="thread-post-content">You issue is you tick rate it too fast. What is the frequency of your crystal? Maybe 8MHz? At 100Khz you are telling an ISR to execute every 10us. If you only have a prescaler rate of 8us &#40;prescaler is set to Fcpu/64 in the port.c&#41; then all you can ever do is call that routine. However, that routine pushes and pops 32 registers + the PC, and the SREG &#8230; think it can do that in 2us and still send serial? Nope, as soon as it&#8217;s done, the flag is already set and it jumps back into the ISR. At 8Mhz the Fcpu tick time is 125ns &#40;at 16MHz it&#8217;s 62.5ns&#41;. Each instruction &#40;and I don&#8217;t know the actual values&#41; can translate to 2 or more clock ticks. Let&#8217;s say each push and pop takes only 2 clock ticks &#8230; 10us/250ns = 40 instructions in 10us.
100KHz seems so out of whack with how fast serial can be sent anyway. 115200Baud = 9bits in 78.1us or 1 byte in 625us. Don&#8217;t go over 1000 for your clock tick rate and think about something even less. I use 400 and can service a PID and trig function fast enough to keep locked on target all the while generating motor speed commands based on the trig while the PID locks the speeds of two motors tot he commanded values all while sending a stream of serial &#40;115200baud&#41; at 10Hz &#40;100ms&#41;. 
How many times per second you you really need to update your PID loop? Does the feedback signal change appreciably during that time? I&#8217;m using 50Hz on mine and it&#8217;s working fine. Does your system react faster than 50Hz &#40;20ms&#41;? 
FWIW,
Jay
