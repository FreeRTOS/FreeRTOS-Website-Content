</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">portENTER_CRITICAL in xSerialPutChar </h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/richardbarry">richardbarry</a> on September 28, 2008</div><div class="thread-post-content">I don&#8217;t know which comtest you are referring to without doing a search for this code.&nbsp; They are all different, but are only meant to demonstrate different features and not as good examples of how an efficient interrupt controller could be written.&nbsp; In fact they are normally deliberately inefficient and designed to create as many interrupts as possible in order to test the port.
Generally if a critical section is used it is to prevent race conditions on one or more of the variables or UART registers.&nbsp; In this case it might be to protect the lTaskWaiting variable.
In most of the ports teach task maintains its own critical nesting and interrupt status.&nbsp; It is therefore possible to yield from a critical section to a task that has interrupts enabled, with the intention of ensuring that interrupts are disabled when the original task next executes.&nbsp; Newer ports tend not to support this behaviour because they use a different mechanism to perform the context switch.
