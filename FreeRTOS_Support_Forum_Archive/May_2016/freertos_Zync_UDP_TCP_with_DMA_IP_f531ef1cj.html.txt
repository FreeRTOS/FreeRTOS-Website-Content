</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">Zync UDP/TCP with DMA IP</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/heinbali01">heinbali01</a> on May 25, 2016</div><div class="thread-post-content">It is not clear to me why you wouldn&#8217;t get DMA interrupts in the merged application.
I&#8217;m afraid I can not help you with device specific settings such as <code>XPAR_CCMAXIREGISTER_0_xxx</code> and <code>XPAR_AXI_DMA_0_xxx</code>.
Note that DMA and memory caching aren&#8217;t friends. Before passing memory to the DMA controller, you have to flush the memory, and after reading to cached memory, you&#8217;ll have to refresh it.
In the FreeRTOS+TCP driver for Zynq, it was decided to reserve 1 MB of SDRAM as uncached memory (see <code>src/uncached_memory.c</code>). Depending on the size that you need, you might want to call:
<pre><code>uint8_t *pucGetUncachedMemory( uint32_t ulSize );
</code></pre>
and get a chunk of aligned / uncached memory.
+TCP is also using DMA. What happens if you don&#8217;t initialise the stack, i.e. never call <code>FreeRTOS_IPInit()</code>? Do you get your DMA interrupt?
<blockquote>
  In the DMA Handler I simply write to the UDP server that the interrupt was processed
</blockquote>
If this DMA Handler is called as an interrupt, it is likely to get into trouble.
FreeRTOS+TCP API&#8217;s may only be called from within a normal task code, not from an interrupt context (with the exception of a few fromISR() functions).
When using FreeRTOS (+TCP), I would let the DMA handler send a message, or give to a semaphore, or notify a task:
~~~~
    xQueueSendFromISR()
    xSemaphoreGiveFromISR()
    xTaskNotifyFromISR()
~~~~
Immediately after the interrupt is finished, the DMA-handling task will wake-up on either:
~~~~
    xQueueReceive()
    xSemaphoreTake()
    xTaskNotifyWait()
~~~~
Please define &#8220;configASSERT_DEFINED = 1&#8221; while testing. See the comments in <code>vPortValidateInterruptPriority()</code> in <code>portable/GCC/ARM_CA9/port.c</code> for an explanation why.
Regards.

  broke the USB port on the board

I&#8217;m sorry to hear that. The USB connector is indeed very delicate.
How do you put new firmware on your device? With an SD-card?
Can&#8217;t you ask your boss for a replacement print? That may be a lot quicker and therefore cheaper 🙂
Do you have <code>UDPLoggingPrintf.c</code> working? It starts a low-priority task that collects logging lines and sends them as UDP messages to a predefined IP-address and port. Every log-line is preceded by a timestamp with uS precision, such as:
~~~~
    0.000.599 [SvrWork] prvServerWorkTask: Creating files
    0.000.732 [SvrWork] Mount SD-card
    0.001.491 [SvrWork] SD card V2
    0.006.225 [SvrWork] sd<em>decode</em>csd: speed 25000000
    0.008.282 [SvrWork] 4-bit bus width enabled.
    0.009.688 [SvrWork] SD/MMC card ready
~~~~
Note that <code>lUDPLoggingPrintf()</code> may not be called from an interrupt context.
The standard gcc <code>s[v][n]printf()</code> functions also cause problems when called from an interrupt.
If you use a later version of <code>printf-stdarg.c</code> (>= 160112), <code>s[v][n]printf()</code> <strong>is</strong> interrupt-safe.
Regards.
