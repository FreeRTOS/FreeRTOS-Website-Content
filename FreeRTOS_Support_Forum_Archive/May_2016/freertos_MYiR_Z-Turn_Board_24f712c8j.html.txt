  <main class="main-content"><div class="thread-post"><h2 class="thread-post-title">MYiR Z-Turn Board</h2><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/raymadigan">raymadigan</a> on May 27, 2016</div><div class="thread-post-content">I have ruined the usb connector of the microZed board and can&#8217;t find another one for at least 60 days.  I need to continue to make some progress but I am stuck without the ability to write data to the serial port.
I have a MYiR Tech Z-Turn board, a Xilinx zynq board with different accessories.  I really only need to be able to use the processors and logic the same as on the microZed.  I don&#8217;t have much experience on the kernel at this time, and I&#8217;m just wondering if there is a sense for how difficult this task would be.  
</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">MYiR Z-Turn Board</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/rtel">rtel</a> on May 27, 2016</div><div class="thread-post-content">I am not familiar with that board, so cannot say how easy or otherwise 
the board is to program and debug, but FreeRTOS itself doesn&#8217;t use 
anything outside of the hard wired Cortex-A9 cores so will run on any 
Zynq hardware.
If you have a hardware specification project, then you will be able to 
create a BSP for the standard parts of the hardware and IP in the SDK &#8211; 
then you can create a project that uses FreeRTOS and run it on the 
hardware as there are no dependencies on any other IP.
Regards.
</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">MYiR Z-Turn Board</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/tlafleur">tlafleur</a> on May 28, 2016</div><div class="thread-post-content">Take an old USB cable, cut off the connector and solder it to the board&#8230;. Tape or ty-wrap to the board to stress relieve it&#8230;.
</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">MYiR Z-Turn Board</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/raymadigan">raymadigan</a> on June 1, 2016</div><div class="thread-post-content">Unfortunately, when the connector came off, it pulled a very thin layer of the pc board with it and there is nothing to solder to on the top.  Under the spot where the connection goes through is the microSD card reader.  Good idea, thank you.
</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">MYiR Z-Turn Board</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/tlafleur">tlafleur</a> on June 2, 2016</div><div class="thread-post-content">Trace back the wire with a ohm meter, you will find a point on the board
for the D+ and D- lines, other is Gnd and +5 (not needed if you have
external power) or if a schematic is available use it to find the pins on
the chip or where the series resistors are located&#8230; You should be able to
fix this board in 30 min or less&#8230;
