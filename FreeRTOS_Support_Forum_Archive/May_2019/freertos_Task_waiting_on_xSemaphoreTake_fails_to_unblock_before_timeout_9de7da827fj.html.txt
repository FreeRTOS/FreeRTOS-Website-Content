  <main class="main-content"><div class="thread-post"><h2 class="thread-post-title">Task waiting on xSemaphoreTake fails to unblock before timeout</h2><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/remboooo">remboooo</a> on May 31, 2019</div><div class="thread-post-content">I have a pretty simple setup on a Cortex-M0+ (Atmel SAML21) with a task that&#8217;s waiting for an interrupt to happen. I use a binary semaphore for this purpose. The task is waiting for the semaphore using xSemaphoreTake with a timeout of 1 second (= 1000 ticks in my setup). The interrupt does not do much more than:
~~~
void interrupt() {
    portBASE<em>TYPE highPrioTaskWoken = pdFALSE;
    debug</em>printf(&#8220;give sem&#8221;);
    xSemaphoreGiveFromISR(tx<em>complete</em>semaphore, &amp;highPrioTaskWoken);
    portEND<em>SWITCHING</em>ISR(highPrioTaskWoken);
}
~~~
The function is actually called by a DMA ISR from Atmel Software Framework, but that should not matter; it&#8217;s an ISR.
The weird thing is: the interrupt happens almost immediately after the call to xSemaphoreTake, but the task calling xSemaphoreTake is only resumed when the timeout expires! The call to xSemaphoreTake <em>does</em> return true, so it&#8217;s definitely not legitimitely waiting for the timeout to expire.
If I increase the timeout to 20 seconds, the call blocks for 20 seconds (still returning true).
If I delay the call to xSemaphoreTake by placing a debug printf before it (which takes some time because it outputs over a UART), the semaphore is given before the xSemaphoreTake is called, and it returns immediately (like it should). If i replace the delay by a vTaskDelay(1), the call blocks until the timeout again.
I used the FreeRTOS Viewer in Atmel Studio to see what was going on. During the ISR, before the semaphore is given, this is the state of the tasks:
~~~
NA  IDLE&#8221;       0   0   536878384   536878072   NA  NA  Running
NA  CfgIF&#8221;      1   1   536881320   536880540   NA  NA  Blocked
NA  MeasCtr&#8221;    2   2   536877664   536876836   NA  NA  Blocked
NA  WDog&#8221;       5   5   536875712   536875340   NA  NA  Blocked
NA  Tmr Svc&#8221;    1   1   536878664   536878472   NA  NA  Suspended
NA  Vengabu&#8221;    2   2   536874136   536873248   NA  NA  Suspended
~~~
The &#8220;CfgIF&#8221; task is the one blocking on the semaphore.
After the xSemaphoreGiveFromISR, this becomes:
~~~
NA  IDLE&#8221;       0   0   536878384   536878072   NA  NA  Running
NA  CfgIF&#8221;      1   1   536881320   536880540   NA  NA  Blocked
NA  MeasCtr&#8221;    2   2   536877664   536876836   NA  NA  Blocked
NA  WDog&#8221;       5   5   536875712   536875340   NA  NA  Blocked
NA  CfgIF&#8221;      1   1   536881320   536880540   NA  NA  Ready
NA  Tmr Svc&#8221;    1   1   536878664   536878472   NA  NA  Suspended
NA  Vengabu&#8221;    2   2   536874136   536873248   NA  NA  Suspended
~~~
So the task waiting for the semaphore is now in the list twice? Once &#8220;ready&#8221;, once &#8220;blocked&#8221;.
When I step through the ISR I can see that portEND<em>SWITCHING</em>ISR() is called with &#8220;true&#8221; and sets the PendSV bit. However, in the subsequent PendSV handler no task switching is happening because uxSchedulerSuspended is true. I know too little about the FreeRTOS internals to know if this is supposed to be this way; it&#8217;s true that before the ISR happens, all tasks are either blocked or suspended (apart from the idle task, but I do use tickless idle).
Any clues on what might be going on here?  I was running FreeRTOS 9.0.0, and because I suspected a bug upgraded to 10.2.1, but this made no difference whatsoever.
</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">Task waiting on xSemaphoreTake fails to unblock before timeout</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/rtel">rtel</a> on May 31, 2019</div><div class="thread-post-content">Could always be a memory corruption caused by a stack overflow or
something like that (do you have stack overflow detection turned on?) &#8211;
but my first suggestion would be take the print statement out of the ISR
&#8211; you said yourself it takes time to execute and will either not be
thread safe or use a thread safety mechanism that could mess up the ISR.
 Does the behaviour change without the print statement?
</div></div><div class="thread-post"><h3 class="thread-post-title">Task waiting on xSemaphoreTake fails to unblock before timeout</h3><div class="thread-post-author">Posted by <a href="http://sourceforge.net/users/remboooo">remboooo</a> on June 1, 2019</div><div class="thread-post-content">Thanks for thinking along. I do have configCHECK<em>FOR</em>STACK<em>OVERFLOW set to 2 and the application keeps chugging along just fine, so that doesn&#8217;t seem to be the problem. I only added the debug</em>printf in the ISR after I noticed this happening, so it&#8217;s not the cause of the problem.
