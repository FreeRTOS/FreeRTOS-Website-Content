I am currently running FreeRTOS on a Atmel AT91SAM7S256.&nbsp; 
We use FreeRTOS Queues and Semaphores for inter-thread communications as well as ISR communications.
The problem I am having is, during heavy application loading, I miss TWI interrupts; hence overflows.&nbsp; Further investigations point at xQueueReceive, xQueueSend causing this.&nbsp; My hypothesis is that because queue activity &#40;hence semaphore activity&#41; causes the interrupts to be disabled for 240us, this causes it to miss the TWI RxRdy interrupt; eventually causing the overflow of the hardware.
The Oscilloscope trace of the TWI overflow and associated signals has been captured and is viewable here:
&nbsp; http://www.flickr.com/photo&#95;zoom.gne?id=285266143&amp;size=o
To capture this, I toggled IO lines on different events.
The signal descriptions are as follows &#40;listed down the right hand edge in the image&#41;:

As you can seen the periodic TWI&#95;RxRdy interrupt occurs approx every 200us and CANNOT be invoked while queues are being manipulated.&nbsp; Hence the next TWI interrupt after Interrupts are re-enabled is for the TWI overflow.

&gt; as ISR communications.
&gt; The problem I am having is, during heavy application loading, I miss TWI interrupts;
&gt; hence overflows.&nbsp; Further investigations point at xQueueReceive, xQueueSend
&gt; causing this.&nbsp; My hypothesis is that because queue activity &#40;hence semaphore
&gt; activity&#41; causes the interrupts to be disabled for 240us, this causes it to
&gt; miss the TWI RxRdy interrupt; eventually causing the overflow of the hardware.
240us seems a long time.&nbsp; Are you sending a lot of data, or making lots of calls to send small amounts of data?&nbsp; There maybe a more efficient alternative.
Also, you may be able to use scheduler locking instead of disabling interrupts.&nbsp; When the scheduler is locked interrupts remain enabled, but context switches cannot occur.
&lt;snip&gt; 
&gt; 1. Is it possible to ONLY disable the PIT timer interrupt while queues are being
&gt; manipulated &amp; context switching ?
Any interrupt that can read or write to the queue being manipulated needs to be disabled if the queue is accessed both from a task and an ISR.
&gt; 2. Is there any variations of the queue implementation&nbsp; &#40;even commercial ones&#41;
&gt; , that do not disable the interrupts, that I could use in FreeRTOS ?
FreeRTOS uses the scheduler locking mechanism to ensure interrupt remain enabled while data is being actually copied to the queue &#40;the part that could potentially take the most time&#41;.&nbsp; Most commercial implementations would just disable interrupts, so may perform worse.&nbsp; The downside is that interrupts are then disabled briefly while the scheduler is being unlocked again.
&gt; 3. Is there any other solutions to this problem ?
There may be a more efficient way of doing whatever it is you are doing.&nbsp; For example, using a circular buffer instead of a queue, and waking the the task using a vTaskResume&#40;&#41; once there is data in the buffer, rather than letting the task wake automatically on the queue event.&nbsp; 
The use of the FIQ is the best option if the TWI interrupt does not itself cause a context switch.
